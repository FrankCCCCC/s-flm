HBFM Radial Eigenfunctions
HBFM input features · design note · 2026-10-21

Which radial factor $r(\lVert z\rVert)$ keeps hyperbolic features bounded at the boundary

For features of the separated form $f_{\ell m}(z) = Y_{\ell m}(z/\lVert z\rVert)\, r_{\ell}(\lVert z\rVert)$ on $\mathbb H^3$: the whole family of admissible $r$, in closed form, classified by what it does at the ideal boundary, with every identity checked numerically.

Answer. Every eigenfunction of the separated form is a Poisson-type transform $r_{\mu,\ell}(s)\,Y_{\ell m}(u) \propto \int_{S^2} e^{\mu B_w(z)}\,Y_{\ell m}(w)\,dw$ of HyLa's plane wave $e^{\mu B}$ against a boundary harmonic. The spike lives in the plane wave, not in the eigenvalue: for $0 \le \operatorname{Re}\mu \le n-1$ the transform is bounded by $\max|Y_{\ell m}|$ for every $\ell$, while the point-evaluated plane wave grows like $e^{(n-1)s/2}$ along its own ray.

What the real part of $\mu$ buys. On $\mathbb H^3$ the boundary behaviour is $|r| \sim e^{(\operatorname{Re}\mu - 2)s}$. HyLa's line $\operatorname{Re}\mu = 1$ (real eigenvalue) decays like $e^{-s}$, so the pinned prompt is invisible; $\mu = 2$ is the current harmonic map $f_\ell$, bounded and non-vanishing but with no radial frequency; the line $\operatorname{Re}\mu = 2$, $\mu = 2 + i\lambda$, keeps $|r| \to 1$ at the boundary and adds a radial phase $e^{i\lambda s}$. That line is the natural completion of the current map: HyLa's spectral parameter $\lambda$ without HyLa's spike or its vanishing.

Recommendation. Use $r_{2+i\lambda,\ell}(s)/C(\mu,\ell)$ with a small ladder $\lambda_k = 1/c_k$, $c_k \in \{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$, in place of the six $\tanh(s/c_k)$ channels; $\lambda = 0$ is the current $f_\ell$. Tabulate it with the float64 ODE route below (no special functions at run time). It settles the boundary question; whether it moves the benchmarks is a separate experiment, and the cheapest one is the sudoku cell where the sphere map already wins.

1. Setup: separation of variables and the whiteboard integral

Work on one $\mathbb H^3$ factor with the dimensionless radius $s = \rho/R$ and direction $u \in S^2$ (curvature $-1$ after scaling, exactly as HarmonicFeatures reads the state). In geodesic polar coordinates on $\mathbb H^n$,

$$\Delta = \partial_s^2 + (n-1)\coth s\,\partial_s + \frac{1}{\sinh^2 s}\,\Delta_{S^{n-1}},$$

so $f = Y_\ell(u)\,r(s)$ with $\Delta_{S^{n-1}} Y_\ell = -\ell(\ell+n-2)Y_\ell$ is an eigenfunction, $\Delta f = \mu(\mu-n+1) f$, iff

$$r'' + (n-1)\coth s\; r' - \frac{\ell(\ell+n-2)}{\sinh^2 s}\, r = \mu(\mu-n+1)\, r. \tag{1}$$

HyLa (Yu & De Sa, ICLR 2023, §3) writes the eigenvalue as $\mu(\mu-n+1)$ for the plane wave $e^{\mu\langle\omega,z\rangle_H}$ with any complex $\mu$, and restricts to $\mu = \tfrac{n-1}{2} + i\lambda$ to make it real. Its Theorem A.2 says every eigenfunction is $\int_{\partial B^n} e^{\mu\langle\omega,z\rangle_H}\,dT(\omega)$ for some boundary functional $T$. The whiteboard's second line is that theorem with $dT = Y_{\ell m}(w)\,dw$: in the code's variables, $\langle w, z\rangle_H = B_w(z) = -\log(\cosh s - \sinh s\,\langle u, w\rangle)$, and

$$\mathbb E_{w \sim \mathrm{Unif}(S^2)}\big[e^{\mu B_w(z)}\, Y_{\ell m}(w)\big] = \frac{\Gamma(\mu+\ell)}{\Gamma(\mu)\,(2\ell+1)!!}\; Y_{\ell m}(u)\; r_{\mu,\ell}(s), \tag{2}$$

where $r_{\mu,\ell}$ is the solution of (1) regular at the origin, normalised to $r \approx s^\ell$ there. The direction and the radius separate because the uniform measure is rotation invariant; the constant in (2) is verified below.

Where the spike comes from. $|e^{\mu B_w(z)}| = e^{\operatorname{Re}\mu\, B_w(z)}$ and $B_w(z) \le s$ with equality at $w = u$: the plane wave evaluated at one boundary point is $e^{\operatorname{Re}\mu\cdot s}$ along its own ray ($e^{s}$ for HyLa on $\mathbb H^3$, $10^{15}$ at the pin) and $e^{-\operatorname{Re}\mu\cdot s}$ almost everywhere else. The $\ell=0$ transform $\phi_\mu(s) = \mathbb E_w[e^{\mu B_w}]$ equals $1$ at $\mu = 0$ and at $\mu = n-1$ (the Poisson kernel integrates to one) and is log-convex in real $\mu$, so $\phi_{\operatorname{Re}\mu} \le 1$ on $0 \le \operatorname{Re}\mu \le n-1$ and

$$\big|\mathbb E_w[e^{\mu B_w} Y_{\ell m}(w)]\big| \le \max|Y_{\ell m}|\cdot \phi_{\operatorname{Re}\mu}(s) \le \sqrt{2\ell+1}\qquad (0 \le \operatorname{Re}\mu \le n-1).$$

Averaging HyLa's plane wave against a boundary harmonic removes the spike for the entire strip; outside the strip $|r|$ grows like $e^{(\operatorname{Re}\mu-(n-1))s}$ and nothing can be done about it. So the "boundary condition" in the whiteboard formula is the choice of $\operatorname{Re}\mu$.

2. Closed forms

General $n$ (ball coordinate $t = \tanh(s/2)$, so $1-t^2 = \operatorname{sech}^2(s/2)$):

$$r_{\mu,\ell}(s) = 2^{\ell}\, t^{\ell}\,(1-t^2)^{\mu}\; {}_2F_1\!\left(\ell+\mu,\; \mu+1-\tfrac n2;\; \ell+\tfrac n2;\; t^2\right), \tag{3}$$

symmetric under $\mu \leftrightarrow n-1-\mu$ (same eigenvalue, same regular solution). At $\mu = n-1$ Euler's transformation turns (3) into $2^\ell t^\ell\,{}_2F_1(\ell, 1-\tfrac n2; \ell+\tfrac n2; t^2)$, which for $n=3$ is exactly the $f_\ell$ tabulated in HarmonicFeatures (up to the $2^\ell$ origin normalisation that the boundary normalisation removes).

$\mathbb H^3$, elementary. Conjugating by $\sinh s$ turns the radial operator into a flat one, $\sinh s\,\Delta(\sinh^{-1}s\, g) = g'' - g + \sinh^{-2}s\,\Delta_{S^2} g$, so the $\ell = 0$ solution is a plain exponential and the higher $\ell$ follow by the same Rodrigues step as the spherical Bessel functions:

$$r_{\mu,0}(s) = \frac{\sinh((\mu-1)s)}{(\mu-1)\sinh s},\qquad r_{\mu,\ell}(s) = \frac{(2\ell+1)!!}{\prod_{k=1}^{\ell}\big((\mu-1)^2 - k^2\big)}\; \sinh^{\ell}\! s \left(\frac{1}{\sinh s}\frac{d}{ds}\right)^{\!\ell} \frac{\sinh((\mu-1)s)}{(\mu-1)\sinh s}. \tag{4}$$

With $\mu - 1 = i\lambda$ this is $\sin(\lambda s)/(\lambda \sinh s)$, HyLa's kernel; with $\mu = 2$ it is the harmonic $f_\ell$ (a removable $0/0$ for $\ell \ge 1$). Both exponents of (1) are visible at the boundary:

$$r_{\mu,\ell}(s) = C(\mu,\ell)\, e^{(\mu-2)s} + C(2-\mu,\ell)\, e^{-\mu s} + O\!\big(e^{(\operatorname{Re}\mu-4)s}\big),\qquad C(\mu,\ell) = \frac{(2\ell+1)!!\;\Gamma(\mu-1)}{\Gamma(\mu+\ell)}. \tag{5}$$

For $\operatorname{Re}\mu > 1$ the first term dominates and $r/C(\mu,\ell) \to e^{(\mu-2)s}$; on HyLa's line $\operatorname{Re}\mu = 1$ both terms have the same modulus and $r$ is a standing wave times $e^{-s}$. Combining (2) and (5), the boundary-normalised feature

$$h_{\mu,\ell m}(z) := (\mu-1)\,\mathbb E_{w\sim \mathrm{Unif}}\big[e^{\mu B_w(z)} Y_{\ell m}(w)\big] = Y_{\ell m}(u)\,\frac{r_{\mu,\ell}(s)}{C(\mu,\ell)} \tag{6}$$

equals $f_\ell(s)Y_{\ell m}(u)$ at $\mu=2$ and tends to $e^{(\mu-2)s}\,Y_{\ell m}(u)$ for every $\ell$ when $\operatorname{Re}\mu > 1$. Because $e^{\mu B} = P(z,w)\,e^{i\lambda B}$ for $\mu = 2+i\lambda$, (6) is also $(\mu-1)\,\mathbb E_{w \sim P(z,\cdot)}[e^{i\lambda B_w(z)} Y_{\ell m}(w)]$: the existing Poisson-kernel sampler with a unit-modulus phase per draw, an unbiased estimator with no weight to spike.

A Dirichlet or Neumann condition at a finite radius $R$ (the other reading of "boundary condition") quantises $\lambda$ on HyLa's line, $\lambda_n = n\pi/R$ for $\ell = 0$ since $r_{1+i\lambda,0} = \sin(\lambda s)/(\lambda\sinh s)$; the functions are bounded and vanish at $R$, but keep decaying like $e^{-s}$ beyond it, so the pin is invisible again. It only makes sense if the state were confined to the ball, which it is not.

5. Implementation sketch

The current module tabulates $f_\ell(s)$ on a $1/256$ grid to $s = 36$ and interpolates; the extension is a table of $r_{\mu_k,\ell}(s)/C(\mu_k,\ell)$ over the ladder $\mu_k = 2 + i/c_k$, stored as real and imaginary channels, with radial() unchanged. Three ways to fill it:

  • (3) with ${}_2F_1$: complex parameters, so scipy.special.hyp2f1 cannot be used (real parameters only); mpmath.hyp2f1 can, at roughly a millisecond per point, which is a minute per table.
  • (4) symbolically (sympy, lambdified): exact, but the prefactor $\prod((\mu-1)^2-k^2)$ cancels against the derivative near $\mu = 2$, losing digits for $\lambda \lesssim 0.05$ at $\ell \ge 1$.
  • ODE route (recommended): $g = \sinh s \cdot r$ satisfies the Schrödinger-type equation $g'' = \big[(\mu-1)^2 + \ell(\ell+1)/\sinh^2 s\big]\,g$ with no first-derivative term. Start at $s_0 = 0.01$ from the series $r \approx s^\ell(1 + a s^2)$, $a = \big[\mu(\mu-2) - \ell(\ell+3)/3\big]/(4\ell+6)$, and integrate to $36$ with DOP853 at rtol=1e-12, atol=0; the regular solution dominates forward for $\operatorname{Re}\mu \ge 1$, so this is stable. Divide by $C(\mu,\ell)$ from scipy.special.gamma, which accepts complex arguments. Measured against mpmath: 2.9e-08 absolute error on the unit-scale $r/C$ up to the pin, well under the $10^{-5}$ interpolation error of the grid, and under $0.1$ s per $(\mu,\ell)$.
def radial_table(mu, l, s_grid, s0=0.01):            # r_{mu,l}(s) / C(mu,l), complex128
    a = (mu*(mu-2) - l*(l+3)/3) / (4*l + 6)
    r0 = s0**l * (1 + a*s0**2); dr0 = l*s0**(l-1)*(1 + a*s0**2) + 2*a*s0**(l+1) if l else 2*a*s0
    g0, dg0 = sinh(s0)*r0, cosh(s0)*r0 + sinh(s0)*dr0
    rhs = lambda s, y: [y[1], ((mu-1)**2 + l*(l+1)/sinh(s)**2) * y[0]]
    sol = solve_ivp(rhs, (s0, s_grid[-1]), [g0+0j, dg0+0j], method='DOP853', rtol=1e-12, atol=0, dense_output=True)
    C = dfact(2*l+1) * gamma(mu-1) / gamma(mu+l)
    return array([sol.sol(s)[0]/sinh(s) if s >= s0 else s**l*(1+a*s*s) for s in s_grid]) / C

Real features: either both quadratures $\operatorname{Re}h$, $\operatorname{Im}h$ per $(\lambda,\ell,m)$, or HyLa's trick $\operatorname{Re}(e^{-ib}h)$ with a phase $b$. Keep $\lambda \le 1$: the oscillating term is what made HyLa's interior states steep (the TinyStories horosphere collapses at $\lambda \sim N(0,1)$), and $|\partial_s h| \lesssim \lambda\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}\,\sqrt{2\ell+1}$ for the phase family. The Cartesian radial gradient $\partial s/\partial z \sim e^{s}$ is a property of the coordinates that every radial feature shares, including the present $\tanh$ channels; the pin is already clamped.

6. What to expect

  • This answers the whiteboard question: the admissible $r$ are the $r_{\mu,\ell}$ with $0 \le \operatorname{Re}\mu \le 2$, and only $\operatorname{Re}\mu = 2$ is both bounded and non-vanishing at the boundary. The two unused dimensions of the family are the real part (decay rate, monotone) and the imaginary part (radial frequency, oscillating).
  • It does not by itself promise a better model. The sphere map ties the coordinates on both benchmarks; its only win is sudoku naive at LR 5e-4 ($+5$ points). TinyStories bridges live at $s \lesssim 2.5$, where a $\lambda \le 1$ phase has rotated less than $2.5$ rad and carries about the same information as the $\tanh$ ladder. The cheapest test is that sudoku cell with the six $\tanh$ channels replaced by the $\lambda$-ladder at $L = 4$.
  • The HyLa line integrated against $Y_{\ell m}$ keeps HyLa's kernel interpretation (real eigenvalue, the addition theorem over $m$) with no spike; it is the right object if the goal is a hyperbolic kernel feature map on bounded states, but it cannot see the pin, which is why it is not the recommendation here.
